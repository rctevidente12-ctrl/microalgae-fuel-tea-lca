Algae fuel from San Carlos flue gas: cost and climate in one screen

Move the inputs to see the minimum fuel selling price (MFSP), the life-cycle greenhouse-gas intensity (GWP), capital and output of a microalgae fuel plant in San Carlos City, Negros Occidental. Cost, GHG and capital come from neural networks trained on 16,000 runs of the full process, techno-economic and ecoinvent 3.12 life-cycle model; annual output is calculated exactly. On held-out runs the networks agree with that model to within the mean errors shown below (about 1% for cost and GHG, 2–3% for capital); this is agreement with the model, not accuracy against a real plant.

What sits behind the numbers

The engine

  • Open raceway ponds (Chlorella vulgaris) fed flue-gas CO₂ from an 18 MW bagasse power plant; settler–membrane–centrifuge dewatering to 20% solids.
  • Route A: hydrothermal liquefaction at 350 °C and 20.9 MPa, then hydrotreating to renewable diesel and naphtha. Route B: wet hexane extraction, transesterification, residue to anaerobic digestion and CHP.
  • Bottom-up capital from NREL and PNNL design reports, CEPCI 2025 = 813 (USD 2025), Southeast Asian proxy location factor; operating prices of 2026; real, unlevered 30-year DCF at 10%; six-year income-tax holiday; tax losses carried forward five years.
  • Background inventory: ecoinvent 3.12 cut-off; one whole-system energy-allocation factor (indicative, not a RED II compliance calculation); functional unit 1 MJ of fuel (LHV).

The surrogate

A 64-64 multilayer perceptron per output, trained on Latin-hypercube samples of 17 inputs (productivity 8–40 g/m²/d) and tested on 20% held-out runs. In a separate test, a model trained only below 25 g/m²/d had larger errors above 30 g/m²/d; the model used here is trained over the full range, and the sliders stop at the trained limits. The networks see all inputs, so they can react slightly to inputs that have no physical effect (approximation error); for that reason output is calculated exactly rather than by the network. Output metrics in the table are those of the network, shown for reference.

Reading the result

  • CME benchmark: coconut methyl ester at 2.22 USD per litre (August 2026), equal to 2.44 USD per litre diesel-equivalent. It is an economic benchmark only: HTL renewable diesel is not an ester, and its eligibility for the biodiesel mandate is not assumed.
  • Diesel benchmark: 1.29 USD per litre, before excise and VAT.
  • GHG thresholds: RED II sets 50%, 60% or 65% savings depending on when an installation started, against a regulatory fossil comparator of 94 g CO₂e/MJ. Meeting a number here is not regulatory compliance.
  • This is a screening tool. The project database holds exported results only; decisions should use the full Python model in the model code package.
  • The low-carbon preset changes the emission factor and heat source only; it keeps the grid electricity price, so it is not scenario S27 or S32.
  • Pond areas above about 1,150 ha need more CO₂ than the 18 MW San Carlos plant emits; larger designs assume a second free biogenic CO₂ source.
